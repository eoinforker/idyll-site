Autumn, 2026Idyll: social without the scroll

Dear you,

I’ve been meaning to write. You know when you open Instagram to answer one message, and twenty minutes later you’re watching a stranger make pasta in a field? I made something about that. It’s called Idyll, and it’s social without the scroll.

It takes the ads, the Shorts, the Reels and the feeds that decide what you watch next out of YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and X. I went through them with a red pen:

YouTube
video ads, Shorts, the recommended home feed, Up next
Instagram
sponsored posts, the Reels feed, the Explore grid
Facebook
Reels, Watch, the algorithmic feed
X
For you, promoted posts, trends, who to follow (that felt good)

What stays is the reason you went in. Your messages. Search. Whatever a friend sends you: if Sam sends you a video, you get that one video, and the feed after it never turns up. Your YouTube subscriptions stay, X Following stays, your Facebook friends stay in order, and so do your groups.

On your iPhone, it’s a Safari content blocker that tidies the websites. Safari applies the rules itself, and iOS will show you that Idyll can’t read or send anything from the pages you visit. If you like, Idyll Plus also skips video ads and turns Shorts back into normal videos. The apps themselves get a daily allowance through Screen Time: they work as usual, notifications and all, until your minutes run out, and then they rest until midnight.

On Android you get a clean built-in browser for each site, and a feed guard inside the real apps that presses “Skip ad” for you and stops the swipe to the next Short or Reel. The one your friend sent still plays. On Chrome and a Mac, the same rules work on your laptop.

A word on privacy, because you’d ask. You log in to the real sites and apps, never inside Idyll. There’s no account. Nothing leaves your phone. There are no trackers, not one.

And I should be honest about the limits, because a friend would be. No app can change what’s inside the official YouTube or Instagram apps on an iPhone; Apple keeps apps sealed off from each other. Anyone who says otherwise is shipping a modified copy, and that can get your account flagged. So Idyll cleans the websites and gives the apps a daily limit instead.

Last thing, and my favourite: it’s free, for good. No subscription, no trial that turns into one, no ads, no data sold. Idyll will never charge you. That was the first decision I made, and the easiest.

Write back soon. Or better, message me. I’ll actually see it.

With love,Idyll
