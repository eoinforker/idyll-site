No subscription. No trial that turns into one. No ads. No data sold. Idyll will never charge, because a thing that gives you your evenings back shouldn't send you a bill for them.

iPhone

A Safari content blocker cleans the websites. Safari applies the rules itself, and iOS shows that Idyll can't read or send anything from the pages. The optional Idyll Plus extension also skips video ads and turns Shorts into normal videos.

The real apps get a daily allowance through Screen Time. They work, notifications and all, until the minutes run out, then pause until midnight.

Android, Chrome and Mac

On Android: a clean built-in browser for each site, plus a feed guard inside the real apps that presses Skip ad and stops the swipe to the next Short or Reel. The one a friend sent still plays.

On Chrome and Mac: the same rules on a laptop.

Your logins, and the honest bit

You log in to the real sites and apps, never inside Idyll. No account. Nothing leaves your phone. Zero trackers.

No app can change what's inside the official YouTube or Instagram apps on iPhone: Apple keeps apps sealed off. Anyone claiming otherwise ships a modified copy that can get accounts flagged. So Idyll cleans the websites and gives the apps a daily limit instead.
